---
title: Pain Tolerance as a Curve
date: 2026-06-07
---

I just ran the [Lake Tahoe Relay](https://laketahoerelay.com/course-info/) with some friends from Thacher XC. For some god-awful reason, and despite explicitly saying that I'd rather do any other segment, I was put on the 6th leg. The route goes from Meeks to Emerald Bay, gaining about 1,200 ft over 8.7 miles. I ran the first bit conservatively and picked it up on the back half for the two largest hills, on which I experienced some of the most profound running-related discomfort of my life.

This morning, the day after the race, I went on a 20-mile trail run quite comfortably (although I did start to get some stomach cramps towards the end). It hurt, but significantly less. While showering after this run, I began to think about how I respond to different magnitudes and types of pain. Here are my thoughts:

## The graph

Each person has a function that takes a certain magnitude of pain + their mental state and maps it to the duration they are able to willingly endure that pain. The graph of that function, for most people in most contexts with most types of pain, would look something like a monotonically decreasing curve. This space could be generalized to much higher dimensions with different mental contexts and different types of pain, and the curve of tolerance could be generalized to a surface over the new space. You would then be able to come up with specific metrics to measure toughness (e.g., general toughness might be the hypervolume under the surface, ultra-running-specific toughness might be over a specific set of regions of contexts/types of pain/intensity of pain, etc.). Obviously this is just a way of thinking about pain tolerance and would be silly/very hard/inaccurate to formalize.

## My curve in the context of running

I think the slope of my curve is significantly less aggressive than it is for most people on the long tail (i.e., I can endure moderate/mild pain for much longer than most people), but that the peak is lower and the initial slope is steeper (I'm a little bitch when it comes to extremely painful events like the hilly segment I just ran at Tahoe). Note that this is relative to the people who run the fast events — I'm confident that I'm tougher than the general population across the spectrum of magnitudes when it comes to running, mostly because I've practiced it.

## Questions this prompts

- Does training this curve in certain contexts improve it in others? I'm inclined to say yes, but that's just based on anecdotal evidence and vibes.
- How can I get better at enduring the middle distances?
- Would improving my pain tolerance there also improve my ultra-running? Again inclined to say yes with the same reasoning.
- Is the gap in my relative performance due to conditioning as opposed to pain tolerance? Probably a bit of both, if I had to guess.
