---
title: Our Perceptual Slice of Reality
date: 2026-05-28
---

Humans have a pretty standard set of sensory organs that hook up to our central processor. How all that information gets integrated into our conscious experience is an open scientific question (The Binding Problem), but the door is closing or already shut on such explanations for most sensory cells themselves.

This is to say that we broadly understand how transduction works for each of the many sensory cells in our body. They're super neat mechanisms, and I'm grateful that mine work properly.

This slice of reality that our brains mysteriously transform into an integrated conscious experience, however, is quite narrow. There is an overwhelming amount of information we can't sense, from the breadth of the electromagnetic spectrum to additional flavor molecules. Additionally, the fidelity of the senses that we do possess pales in comparison to that of many other species ([Mantis shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantis_shrimp#Eyes) have possibly the coolest eyes, and I want them). There is so much more to the world than we are able to experience, thanks to our rather limited set of sensory cell types.

I believe that, at some point in the near future, we will be able to engineer, either directly (seems iffy) or genetically (how I think it'll work), these receptors. I don't know if I'll be around at that point (or if we'll be able to put them in adults or if my brain would be able to accept them), but it'd be incredible to expand that narrow slice of reality to which my mind has direct access.
